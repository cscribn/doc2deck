  • Top 10% is Appfire's AI-assisted performance review cycle: one document per person, five milestones, two AI agents.
  • The name comes from the end of the cycle: senior leadership draws a line so the top tier stays small, roughly the top 10%. Leadership sets it by judgment each cycle, aiming at roughly 10%.
  • What changes from the old practice: documents are coached against one rubric, an AI score is the starting point for calibration, and calibration ranks people instead of changing their grades.

One document per person AI agent · advisory
  • M1 Self-evaluation: the employee writes a self-evaluation; colleagues write peer reviews and upward reviews for their bosses.
  • M2 Manager evaluation: the manager collects evidence and fills in the template, helped by the Coach (AI agent 1), then submits a 9-box placement.
  • M3 Internal calibration: peer managers align with their Director; Director, Senior Manager and VP comments and score changes go into the document, which is then Completed.
  • M4 Calibration: the Scorer (AI agent 2) scores every completed document; cross-department groups rank their people.
  • M5 Senior leadership: Director+ draw the line on the top tier.
  • The same document travels through every milestone. Both agents are advisory: people decide.
Step 1.1

The employee writes a self-evaluation

  • Who: the employee. When: the cycle opens.
  • Describe what they delivered, how, its impact and complexity, and where they want to grow. Link the evidence: Jira issues, pull requests, pages.
  • It's an input, not a verdict: the Coach and the Scorer treat every statement as a claim to verify.
  • Output: a submitted self-evaluation, before the M1 deadline.
Step 1.2

Colleagues review each other, and their bosses

Manager

  • Nominated reviews: the manager nominates up to 2 peer reviewers per person.
  • Open feedback: on top of that, anyone can write feedback for anyone, colleagues and bosses alike (upward reviews). Runs in parallel with the self-evaluation.
  • Good feedback describes specific behavior and outcomes, with both strengths and growth areas.
  • Growth areas raised by peers weigh as much as their praise (rubric rule).
  • M1 ends when the self-evaluation and reviews are in. The manager takes over.
Step 2.1

The manager collects the evidence

Evidence
  • Who: the manager. M2 starts once M1 is done.
  • From people: the self-evaluation, peer and upward reviews, 1:1 notes and the manager's own observations.

  • Who: the manager runs the Coach on the draft, as often as they like. It's at the manager's disposal.
  • It checks: missing or weak evidence per criterion, claims the systems contradict, unclear attribution between person and team, significant work the document doesn't mention, vague wording, empty sections, complexity of key work against the position.
  • It returns: findings and follow-up questions. The manager edits; the loop repeats until the manager is satisfied.
  • It never decides or suggests the 9-box, a rating, promotion or pay, and it shows no score.
  • Reads Jira, Confluence, Bitbucket and GitHub directly; information from Figma, Jellyfish and Workday comes from what the manager entered by hand. Builds on the existing Rovo Performance Assessor.

Starting point for ranking
  • When: once documents are Completed, before calibration sessions. Run by: HR. Seen by: managers and calibration participants, not the employee.
  • Reads: the completed document, every comment and score change, and verifies claims in Jira, Confluence, Bitbucket and GitHub. Finds significant work the document doesn't mention.

  • The score is the starting point for the ranking, not the final word. It never changes the grade.

Steps 5.1 – 5.2

Leadership decides where the line falls

Director +
Top three boxes One box down The line
  • Who: senior leadership, Director and above. HR consulted.
  • Input: all ranked lists from the calibration groups, merged in rank order (stripe color = source group).
  • Leadership decides by judgment, each cycle, where the line falls on the top-graded employees: the top three boxes (Consistent star, Rising star, High impact performer). The aim is roughly the top 10%.
  • Top-graded people below the line move one box down. Rising star and High impact performer → Key contributor; Consistent star → Rising star or High impact performer, at leadership's choice. The final box is recorded on every document.
  • Only the top-graded group is cut; the other boxes aren't.
